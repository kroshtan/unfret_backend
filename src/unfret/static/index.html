The questions to ask before you pay

Unfret checks the page for you with these in mind, and tells you what it found and where it found it.

  • Will it arrive?

    Who is really selling, how long the shop has been around, and whether its address and company details hold up.

  • Am I protected?

    How you pay and whether you can get your money back: a card or a platform's buyer protection, or a bank transfer you can't undo.

  • Am I being pushed?

    Countdowns that start again, stock that never runs out, discounts on a price nobody paid, and reviews that weren't written here.

  • Is it worth the price?

    Generic goods under a made-up brand, the same photo on other shops for less, costs that show up only at checkout.

  • Does it work?

    Claims that contradict each other or the photos, and specs that can't be true.

  • Is it any good?

    Signs of throwaway quality: the hardest to tell from a page, so Unfret says what it saw and leaves the call to you.

How a result reads

One risk level, from 0 to 4: the higher, the worse. It always comes with its reasons, worst first.

  1. 4{{level_4}}. The shop may not be real, or your card or personal details may be at risk.
  2. 3{{level_3}}. Claims that don't add up.
  3. 2{{level_2}}. Markups, hidden costs, costly returns.
  4. 1{{level_1}}. Fake urgency, fake discounts, fake reviews.
  5. 0{{level_0}}. Nothing worrying on the page.

How you pay counts too: a card or a platform's buyer protection can lower the level; a bank transfer, crypto or gift cards can raise it. Several smaller problems together can add up to a higher level.

Tricks Unfret looks for

The same few tricks turn up on shop after shop. Each one is easy to miss when you're about to pay.

  • Sold by another seller · ships from overseas

    Who's really selling

    On a marketplace, the seller behind the listing is often a stranger, not the platform.

    Unfret looks at the seller: company details, a real address, and how long they've been around.

  • Sale ends in 09:59

    A countdown that starts again

    “Offer ends in 10 minutes”, and when you come back tomorrow the clock is back at ten minutes.

    Unfret notices timers that restart and the tools shops use to add them.

  • We're closing down: everything must go!

    The closing-down story

    A shop that's always in its last days, retiring or clearing stock, so you buy before it's “gone”.

    Unfret spots never-ending closing-down sales and other pressure in the page's words.

  • $189.99 $39.99 −79%

    A “was” price nobody paid

    A big discount measured against a price the shop never charged, or a “recommended” price.

    Unfret compares the “was” price with the prices it has seen for that product, and flags discounts too deep to be true.

  • Free shipping · delivery 15–30 business days

    Shipped from far away, in the small print

    The shop looks local, but the goods come from overseas: weeks of waiting, and returns sent abroad.

    Unfret reads the shipping and returns pages for delivery times and where returns go.

  • ★★★★★ “Great product!” ×3

    Reviews that were never written here

    The same review word for word, reviews imported from other sites, or more ratings than a new product could have.

    Unfret looks for repeated and imported reviews, and reviews it has seen on other shops.

  • Brand: XQZEUL

    Factory goods under a made-up brand

    A generic product with a brand nobody has heard of, often with the same photo as on other shops, at several times the price.

    Unfret checks whether the brand exists and whether the photo appears on other shops.

  • A family business since 1998

    “Since 1998”, online since last month

    A long history on the page, on a web address that was registered a few weeks ago.

    Unfret looks up when the address was registered and compares it with the story.

The examples above are made up; the tricks are real, seen again and again on real shops. Unfret describes what a page shows and lets you decide: it never calls a shop names.

    Questions

    Does Unfret tell me a shop is a scam?

    No. Unfret never calls a shop a scam. It gives a risk level from 0 to 4 and shows what it noticed on the page and where that comes from. It can miss a bad shop, and it can warn about a good one, so the decision is yours. See how accurate it is.

    Which sites does it work on?

    Products on marketplaces such as Amazon and on web shops of any size, second-hand ads, and the websites of tradespeople and local services. Some big shops turn automatic checks away; for those, use the browser extension or the Android app, which read the page in your own browser.

    What happens to the links I check?

    Unfret reads the one page you ask about, only when you ask. The links you check aren't stored and are never linked to you. A "Wrong result?" report keeps that one page, with personal details removed, and not who sent it. The privacy statement has the details.

    Why not just ask an AI assistant?

    A capable assistant can do much of a careful check if you ask it well, and it makes a good second opinion. Unfret adds records no assistant has (prices, product photos and shops it has seen before) and gives the same answer for the same evidence, with the source of every finding: Unfret compared.

    Do I need an account?

    No. Checks are free and need no account, no pass, no name and no email: nothing ties a check to you.

    How does Unfret make money?

    Shoppers' checks run on donations; developers and AI agents pay for theirs. Nothing else: no ads, no selling data, no affiliate links to shops. How Unfret makes money.

    Who makes Unfret?

    Floris van Beers, a one-person company in the Netherlands (Kroshtan). The code is open source under the MIT licence, and the language model that reads pages runs in the EU, at Scaleway. About Unfret. Organizations that protect consumers are welcome to get in touch.
