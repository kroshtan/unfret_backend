Privacy

Last updated: 16 November 2026. Unfret is made by Kroshtan (Floris van Beers, the Netherlands).

What a check sends

Nothing leaves your browser until you click Check this page in the extension (or an agent calls /v1/check). Then the page address, the product facts (title, brand, seller, price, ratings, reviews, shop policies) and the page's visible text go to our server in the EU. Account menus, addresses, forms and anything you typed are removed in your browser first.

On a service company's own site or a provider's profile on a platform (a plumber, a locksmith, a Werkspot or Thumbtack profile) the check also sends what the business publishes about itself: its name, KvK or licence number, address, phone numbers and e-mail addresses (from its footer, contact and legal sections and its structured data), its prices and payment terms, the towns it lists, and its testimonials (the text, the name as the site prints it, the rating and the date). On a platform profile: the platform's figures (member since, jobs, rating, badges, response rate) and its reviews, without the reviewers' names. These are used for the check only.

On a private seller's ad (Marktplaats, 2dehands, Vinted, Craigslist, Facebook Marketplace, or a private seller on eBay) a check also reads the ad's own text, the item details (price, condition, category, delivery, location) and what the platform shows about the seller's account: since when, ratings, badges, other ads, reply time. Never the seller's name. All of this is used for that one check and then dropped, except the fingerprints described below.

The link checker sends only the link, when you tap Check. Our server then fetches the page itself, so the shop sees our server, not you. A link shared to Unfret from another app stays in your browser until you tap Check. The page keeps no history; only your Unfret pass (or a wallet's sign-in) stays in your browser, until you remove it on My checks, and a note that this browser has had its free pass.

Free checks. When you install the extension, or tap Check on the link checker for the first time, it asks our server for a new Unfret pass with a few free checks. That request carries nothing about you or the pages you visit. To give free checks only once, the extension remembers in your browser's own extension storage that it got them (your browser may sync that note between your devices, as it does settings), and the link checker in this browser's storage; neither note says anything else. Our server counts new free passes per internet connection for a day, in memory only and under a salted one-way hash of the address, gone at the next restart: it never stores your IP address. Of the free checks it stores only how many a pass has left, and how many were used in all today.

Your Unfret pass is what holds your checks (developers know it as an API key). When the checkout page or My checks gives you a pass, it can hand it to the Unfret extension in the same browser, so you don't have to paste it there: the pass goes from the page to the extension only, in your browser, and nowhere else. If the extension uses another pass or wallet, the page asks you first. The extension reads nothing else from the page and tells it only whether it took the pass. Use on your phone on My checks shows a QR code with a link to the link checker that carries your pass after a #, a part of a link browsers never send to any server; the phone keeps the pass like a pasted one. The code is made on our server and not kept.

What we keep

  • Your wallet and its balance: the Solana address you signed in with and how many checks you have left. No name, e-mail or password.
  • Or your Unfret pass and its balance: the pass's public ID, its balance, its free checks left, and one-way hashes of the pass and its backup code. We never store either in readable form, so keep the backup code: we can't recover the pass for you.
  • Card payments: Polar sells the pack to you as merchant of record. It collects your payment details, e-mail and country, charges VAT and sends the receipt, under its own privacy policy. We tell Polar only the pack and the pass's public ID. We keep the order number, the amount and the pass it topped up for 180 days, for refunds and questions; never your name, e-mail or address. If your bank app brings you back to another browser after paying, Polar's return link names the payment, and that browser can collect your checks once, within a day, as a new pass (the one made where you started then stops working); we note only that it happened, and when.
  • Gift codes: for each gift code that comes with a pack, a one-way hash, the day it was issued and the day it was used, and a keyed one-way hash of the pass or wallet that bought it, so it can't be used on that one (a gift is for a friend). Not who used it, no IP address: nothing links the buyer to the friend who used it. The codes are shown once, on your payment confirmation, and wiped from our server as they are shown (unshown codes go with the purchase record within a week). Until then the checkout page keeps a random claim token for them in your browser, for up to three days. An account that used a gift code is marked as having used one, not which.
  • Affiliates and bonus codes: if you came through an affiliate's link (unfret.net/?ref=…) or used their code, this website keeps that code in your browser's own storage (no cookie, nothing sent anywhere) until you get a pass; then we note, with the pass's public ID, which affiliate it came through and when, and the day it made its first check. Nothing else: no IP address, no pages, nothing about your browser. That is what gives you bonus checks on your first pack and pays the affiliate a share of what is paid on that pass, for a year. Affiliates see only totals per month (passes, first checks, purchases, amounts), never a pass, a buyer or what anyone checked. The note goes after 18 months; the commission records (amounts, the order number or transaction, the day) stay for seven years for our bookkeeping. A bonus code you use is counted, not linked to you. See the affiliate program.
  • Not the pages you check. Page addresses and contents aren't stored and are never linked to your wallet or pass. A result stays in memory for up to an hour, so checking the same page again is free; a restart clears it. The only results we keep are ones you choose to share, without any link to you (below).
  • Price history: for product pages with a public product number (an Amazon ASIN, a bol.com ID, a barcode), the day, the price and the claimed "was" price, under a one-way hash of the shop's domain and that number. No address, wallet, IP address or time of day. Kept 90 days, to show whether a "was" price was ever really charged.
  • Prices across shops: for product pages with a barcode, the day and the price under a one-way hash of the barcode and another of the shop's domain (a marketplace counts as one shop), to see whether a price is far below what other shops ask for the same product. No address, wallet, IP address or time of day. Kept 90 days. A private seller's ad naming a barcode is compared with these prices; prices in ads are never recorded.
  • Review fingerprints: for the longer reviews on a checked product page, a one-way hash of each review's text (in lower case, letters and digits only) and of the shop's domain, with the first and last day we saw it, to spot the same reviews on different shops. Never the review itself, the reviewer's name or the page. Kept 90 days after we last saw it.
  • Shop facts: a shop domain's registration date, first certificate and VAT check, and whether a product's brand is on Wikidata, kept a week so they aren't looked up again for every shopper.
  • Shop fingerprints: for web shops and service companies' own websites (not marketplaces or platforms), what their pages reveal about the business behind them: the first and last day we saw the site, its platform and apps, the shape of its template, and the account IDs on its pages (analytics, ad pixels, payment accounts). This recognises shops run by the same operator, and one call centre behind many "local" service brands. Never who checked the site, which page, or our verdict. Kept 400 days after we last saw the site.
  • Product photo fingerprints: for the main photo of a checked product (on a service company's page, its first photo that isn't a logo), its fingerprints (computed on our server; the photo goes to no one else), the photo's address on the shop, the shop and a one-way hash of the listing, to spot the same photo on another shop. Kept 400 days after last seen. The main photo of a private seller's ad is kept the same way, but only 90 days, and is also compared with other ads, to spot a photo copied from someone else's ad. Matching photos are shown as small thumbnails from our server, so your browser never contacts the other shop.
  • Ad fingerprints: for a private seller's ad, a fingerprint of its text (a 64-bit hash of its words; the text can't be read back from it), the platform, a one-way hash of the ad's address and the day, to spot the same ad posted many times. Phone numbers and e-mail addresses written in the ad are kept only as a keyed one-way hash, to notice one number behind many ads, and are never shown to anyone. No seller name, account, location, price or ad text, and nothing about who checked the ad. Kept 90 days after we last saw the ad.
  • Recent answers for agents: the answer to a paid check of a page our server fetched itself, under a one-way hash of the listing, for one day, so the same unchanged listing can be answered quickly. No address, caller or payment details.
  • Results you share: only if you tap Share this result and confirm the preview. Anyone with the link then sees the verdict in our public wording (no score, no quotes from the page), the product's title, the shop, the product link (without tracking parameters) and the day of the check, for 90 days; then it is deleted. Nothing about who shared it: the share is sent without your sign-in, and we keep no wallet, pass, IP address, time of day or view count with it. You get a delete code once (we keep only its hash) to delete it sooner. Shared pages ask search engines not to list them.

For shop owners and service companies

Contact e-mail addresses, phone numbers and postal addresses on a shop's or service company's pages, and a service company's e-mail domain, are stored only as a keyed one-way hash, never shown to anyone, and used only to tell whether two sites share them, or, for a postal address, how many unrelated shops give the same one (our legitimate interest in warning shoppers about networks of fake shops, virtual offices and call centres posing as local businesses). They are deleted 180 days after we last saw them. A check of a service provider stays with the person who ran it: it can't be shared as a link. To object, or to have a site or contact detail removed, use the contact details at kroshtan.com. A shared result about your page can be reported from the page itself: it is hidden at once until we review it, and what you write is kept only until then.

For private sellers

If someone checked your ad, we keep only the ad fingerprints above: no name, account or address, and nothing that says who checked it. A phone number or e-mail address you put in your ad is kept as a keyed one-way hash for 90 days, used only to tell whether the same number is behind many ads (our legitimate interest in warning buyers about ads posted in bulk). To have it removed sooner, use the contact details at kroshtan.com.

Reporting a wrong result

If you press This is wrong (or an API user sends a report) and agree, that check's page facts, page text and result are shared for review. Personal details are removed in your browser and again on our server: greetings with your name, delivery and e-mail addresses, phone numbers, bank and card numbers, and everything after the ? in links. The checkout page is never kept.

Until it is reviewed, a report is linked to your wallet or pass, only so a good report can earn free checks, and we count your reports per month (as a keyed hash). After review the report is deleted; the page and its label stay, without any link to you, as an example for improving the checks. Reports not reviewed within 30 days are deleted.

Who else sees what

  • A language model hosted by Scaleway in the EU reads the page text. We send it nothing that identifies you.
  • Public registries (RDAP, crt.sh, the EU's VIES) and Wikidata answer the shop and brand lookups. They see the shop's domain, its VAT number, or the product's brand and the domains named after it, not you. The EU's Safety Gate alerts (dangerous products) are a copy on our server: checking a product against them asks no one.
  • Crypto payments are Solana transactions, public on the blockchain like any other.
  • Your country: Cloudflare, which runs in front of this site, sees your IP address like it does for any site it serves; our server gets only the country code it works out, uses it only to suggest a country for prices, payment options and VAT, which you can change at checkout, and never stores it.
  • When you pay with SOL or USDC you choose your country, for VAT. It may be prefilled from the country Cloudflare sees your connection coming from; your IP address is never stored. For our tax records we keep the day, the country, the amount, the VAT and the transaction signature of each purchase for ten years, as the law requires.

Nothing is sold. No affiliate links to shops (Unfret never earns from the shops it checks), no trackers, no ads. This website loads nothing from anyone else: its scripts, styles and typeface (Figtree) come from unfret.net itself, so no font service or CDN sees your visit.

Your choices

Signing out in the extension's settings removes the session from your browser; uninstalling removes everything the extension stored. Questions: see the contact details at kroshtan.com.
