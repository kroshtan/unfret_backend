Privacy

Last updated: 10 November 2026. Unfret is made by Kroshtan (Floris van Beers, the Netherlands).

When anything is sent

Only when you click Check this page in the extension (or an agent asks /v1/check). Then the page address, the product facts (title, brand, seller, price, ratings, review texts, shop policies) and the cleaned visible text go to this server, in the EU. Account menus, addresses, forms and anything you typed are removed in your browser first. Nothing is sent while you browse.

On the link checker only the link goes to this server, when you tap Check; the server then fetches that page from the shop itself (the shop sees this server, not you) and checks it like above. The page keeps no history; only your sign-in or API key is kept in your browser, until you sign out. A link shared to Unfret from another app stays in your browser until you tap Check.

What this server keeps

  • Your wallet and its balance: the Solana address you signed in with and how many checks you have left. No name, email or password.
  • Or your API key and its balance: if you use a key instead of a wallet, a one-way hash of the key and of its recovery code, the key's public ID and its balance. The key itself is shown to you once and never stored, so we can't recover it for you: keep the recovery code. A card payment is handled by Polar, which sells the pack to you as merchant of record: it collects your payment details, e-mail and country, charges VAT and sends the receipt, under its own privacy policy. We give Polar only the pack and the key's public ID, so the payment tops up the right key. We keep only the order number, the amount and which key it topped up, for 180 days (for refunds and questions), never your name, e-mail or address.
  • Invites: a pack comes with invite codes to give away. We keep only a one-way hash of each code, the day it was issued and the day it was used: not who bought it, not who used it, no IP address, so nothing links the buyer to the person they invited. The codes themselves are shown once, on your payment confirmation, and wiped from the server when they are shown (or, if never shown, with the purchase's record after at most a week); the checkout page keeps a random claim token for them in your browser for up to three days, until they are shown. An account that used an invite is marked as having used one, not which.
  • Not the pages you check. Page addresses and contents are not stored and are never linked to your wallet. A result is kept in memory for up to an hour so the same page checked again is free; a restart clears it. Only a result you choose to share is kept, without any link to you (below).
  • Anonymous price observations: for product pages with a public product number (an Amazon ASIN, a bol.com ID or a barcode), the day, the price and the claimed "was" price, under a one-way hash of the shop's domain and that number. No address, wallet, IP address or time of day. They're kept 90 days and show whether a "was" price was ever really charged.
  • Facts about shops: a shop domain's registration date, first certificate and VAT check, for a week, so they aren't looked up again for every shopper.
  • Shop fingerprints: for web shops (not marketplaces), what their pages show about the business behind them: the first and last day a check saw the shop, its platform and apps, the shape of its template, and the account IDs on its pages (analytics, advertising pixels, payment accounts), so shops run by the same operator can be recognized. Never who checked the shop, which page, or our verdict. Kept 400 days after the shop was last seen.
  • Product photo fingerprints: of the main photo of a checked product, its fingerprints (computed on this server; the photo goes to no one else), the photo's own address on the shop, the shop and a one-way hash of the listing, so the same photo on another shop can be recognised. Kept 400 days after last seen. Matching photos are shown as small thumbnails served by this server, so your browser never contacts the other shop.
  • Recent answers for agents: the answer of a paid check of a page this server fetched itself, under a one-way hash of the listing, for one day, so the same listing can be answered again quickly while the shop is unchanged. No address, caller or payment details.
  • "Spotted recently" examples: checks Unfret ran itself on public product pages, to show what a check finds. For each: the product's title, the shop, the page's address, the day it was checked, the result and sometimes a screenshot of the page. Only pages we chose and checked ourselves, never a shopper's own check, and nothing about who checks what. Each one is reviewed before it is published and can be taken down (see below, for shop owners).
  • Results you share: only if you tap Share this result and confirm the preview. Then the verdict in our public wording (no score and no quotes from the page), the product's title, the shop, the product link (without tracking parameters) and the day of the check are public to anyone with the link, for 90 days, after which they are deleted. Nothing about who shared it: the share is sent without your sign-in, and we keep no wallet, key, IP address, time of day or view count with it. You get a delete code once (we keep only a one-way hash of it) to delete it sooner. Shared pages ask search engines not to list them.

For shop owners: contact e-mail addresses and phone numbers shown on a shop's pages are stored only as a keyed one-way hash, never shown to anyone, used only to tell whether two shops share them (our legitimate interest in warning shoppers about networks of fake shops), and deleted 180 days after they were last seen. To object or have a shop or contact detail removed, use the contact details at kroshtan.com. The same goes for a "Spotted recently" example of your shop's page: tell us and we'll look at it again or take it down. A shared result about your page can be taken down from the page itself: it is hidden at once, and we review it; what you write with the objection is kept only until then.

When you report a wrong result

If you press This is wrong (or an API user sends a report) and agree, that one check's page facts, page text and result are shared for review. Personal details are removed in your browser and again on the server: greetings with your name, delivery addresses, e-mail addresses, phone numbers, bank and card numbers, and everything after the ? in links. The checkout page is never kept. While the report waits for review it is linked to your wallet or key, only so a good report can be rewarded with free checks; we also count your reports per month (as a keyed hash). After review the report is deleted and the page, with its label, stays as an example for improving the checks, without any link to you. Reports nobody got to are deleted after 30 days. If the result was right, we may check the same public page again ourselves and, after review, show that new check as a "Spotted recently" example; it carries nothing from your report and no link to you.

Who else sees what

  • The page text is read by a language model hosted by Scaleway in the EU; Unfret sends it nothing that identifies you.
  • Public registries answer the shop lookups (RDAP registries, crt.sh, the EU VIES service). They see the shop's domain or VAT number, not you.
  • Payments are Solana transactions: public on the blockchain, like any payment there.
  • When you buy credits with SOL or USDC you choose your country, for VAT. It may be prefilled from the country Cloudflare sees your connection coming from; your IP address is never stored. For our tax records we keep, per purchase, the day, that country, the amount, the VAT and the transaction's signature, for ten years, as the law requires.

Nothing is sold. There are no affiliate links, trackers or advertising.

Your choices

Sign out in the extension's settings to remove the session from your browser; uninstalling removes everything the extension stored. Questions: see the contact details at kroshtan.com.
