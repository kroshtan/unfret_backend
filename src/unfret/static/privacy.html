Privacy

Last updated: November 22, 2026. Unfret is made by Kroshtan (Floris van Beers, Kopenhagenstraat 2a, 9723 BB Groningen, the Netherlands), who is responsible for the personal data described here (the controller, in the words of the GDPR). Questions and requests: hello@unfret.net.

What a check sends

Nothing leaves your browser until you click Check this page in the extension (or an agent calls /v1/check). Then the page address, the product facts (title, brand, seller, price, ratings, reviews, shop policies) and the page's visible text go to our server in the EU. Account menus, addresses, forms and anything you typed are removed in your browser first, and so is what the address carries about you: the part after #, tracking and referral parameters (such as utm_…, gclid or fbclid), sessions, tokens and e-mail addresses. What picks the product or its size or colour stays.

On a service company's own site or a provider's profile on a platform (a plumber, a locksmith, a Werkspot or Thumbtack profile) the check also sends what the business publishes about itself: its name, KvK or licence number, address, phone numbers and e-mail addresses (from its footer, contact and legal sections and its structured data), its prices and payment terms, the towns it lists, and its testimonials (the text, the name as the site prints it, the rating and the date). On a platform profile: the platform's figures (member since, jobs, rating, badges, response rate) and its reviews, without the reviewers' names. These are used for the check only.

On a private seller's ad (Marktplaats, 2dehands, Vinted, Craigslist, Facebook Marketplace, or a private seller on eBay) a check also reads the ad's own text, the item details (price, condition, category, delivery, location) and what the platform shows about the seller's account: since when, ratings, badges, other ads, reply time. Never the seller's name. All of this is used for that one check and then dropped, except the fingerprints described below.

The link checker sends only the link, when you tap Check. Our server then fetches the page itself, so the shop sees our server, not you. A link shared to Unfret from another app stays in your browser until you tap Check. The page keeps no history; only an Unfret pass you added (or a wallet's sign-in) stays in your browser, until you remove it on My checks.

Free checks. Quick and deep checks are free and need no pass: a free check carries nothing that names you or your device. For a deep check, your browser or app first solves a small puzzle (a proof of work, a few seconds of computing) that our server issues; the puzzle names nothing either. To keep free checks fair, our server counts free deep checks per internet connection for an hour, in memory only and under a salted one-way hash of the address, gone at the next restart: it never stores your IP address for this. It also adds up what the AI model costs us each day, as two numbers (free checks and paid ones), so free deep checks can pause when the day's share of the donations is used up.

Donations. "Support Unfret" sends you to a payment provider's page. What you enter there goes to that provider and, like any payment, shows in our business account (the amount, and the name the provider passes on). It is never linked to anything you check. Our server only counts how many times the donate link is opened in a month, as one number.

Your Unfret pass is what holds your checks (developers know it as an API key). When the checkout page or My checks gives you a pass, it can hand it to the Unfret extension in the same browser, so you don't have to paste it there: the pass goes from the page to the extension only, in your browser, and nowhere else. If the extension uses another pass or wallet, the page asks you first. The extension reads nothing else from the page and tells it only whether it took the pass. Use on your phone on My checks shows a QR code with a link to the link checker that carries your pass after a #, a part of a link browsers never send to any server; the phone keeps the pass like a pasted one. The code is made on our server and not kept.

What we keep

  • Your wallet and its balance: the Solana address you signed in with and how many checks you have left. No name, e-mail or password. Signing in with a wallet gives you a session (a signed token, valid 30 days) that is kept in your browser, not on our server.
  • Or your Unfret pass and its balance: the pass's public ID, its balance, its free checks left, the day it was made (and when it was last replaced from its backup code), and one-way hashes of the pass and its backup code. We never store either in readable form, so keep the backup code: we can't recover the pass for you.
  • If you connect an app to Unfret's MCP server by signing in (Claude's connectors, for example): one-way hashes of the sign-in's codes and tokens, tied to the pass you picked, with the app's client ID and when each expires; for an app that registered itself, the name and return addresses it sent. Nothing about you, your network or what you check. A token lasts an hour, a renewal token 30 days unused; they go when they expire, when you disconnect the app, when you replace the pass with its backup code, or with the pass.
  • How long: a wallet's or pass's balance, and whether it used an invite or code or ever bought checks, are kept for as long as Unfret runs, because checks never expire. To have yours deleted (with the checks left on it), write to us with the pass's ID or the wallet's address.
  • Card payments: Polar sells the top-up to you as merchant of record. It collects your payment details, e-mail and country, charges VAT and sends the receipt, under its own privacy policy. We tell Polar only the amount and the pass's public ID. We keep the order number, the amount and the pass it topped up for 180 days, for refunds and questions; never your name, e-mail or address. If your bank app brings you back to another browser after paying, Polar's return link names the payment, and that browser can collect your checks once, within a day, as a new pass (the one made where you started then stops working); we note only that it happened, and when.
  • Not the pages you check. Page addresses and contents aren't stored, on their own or with your wallet or pass. A result stays in memory for up to an hour. For that hour the server also keeps, in memory only, a one-way code made from your pass (or wallet) and the page, so that opening the same result again with the same pass uses no second check; the code contains neither. A restart clears both. The only results we keep are ones you choose to share, without any link to you (below).
  • Price history: for product pages with a public product number (an Amazon ASIN, a bol.com ID, a barcode), the day, the price and the claimed "was" price, under a one-way hash of the shop's domain and that number. No address, wallet, IP address or time of day. Kept 90 days, to show whether a "was" price was ever really charged.
  • Prices across shops: for product pages with a barcode, the day and the price under a one-way hash of the barcode and another of the shop's domain (a marketplace counts as one shop), to see whether a price is far below what other shops ask for the same product. No address, wallet, IP address or time of day. Kept 90 days. A private seller's ad naming a barcode is compared with these prices; prices in ads are never recorded.
  • Review fingerprints: for the longer reviews on a checked product page, a one-way hash of each review's text (in lower case, letters and digits only) and of the shop's domain, with the first and last day we saw it, to spot the same reviews on different shops. Never the review itself, the reviewer's name or the page. Kept 90 days after we last saw it.
  • Shop facts: a shop domain's registration date, first certificate and VAT check, and whether a product's brand is on Wikidata, kept a week so they aren't looked up again for every shopper.
  • Shop fingerprints: for web shops and service companies' own websites (not marketplaces or platforms), what their pages reveal about the business behind them: the first and last day we saw the site (and the first day a page our server fetched itself showed it, which tells later checks how long the shop has been online: only the shop's domain and that day), its platform and apps, the shape of its template, and the account IDs on its pages (analytics, ad pixels, payment accounts). This recognises shops run by the same operator, and one call centre behind many "local" service brands. Never who checked the site, which page, or our verdict. Kept 400 days after we last saw the site. Account IDs, contact details and addresses from a page your browser or app sent link that site to others only once several independent checks reported the same, or our server fetched a page showing it (see below).
  • Product photo fingerprints: for the main photo of a checked product (on a service company's page, its first photo that isn't a logo), its fingerprints (computed on our server; the photo goes to no one else), the photo's address on the shop, the shop and a one-way hash of the listing, to spot the same photo on another shop. Kept 400 days after last seen. The main photo of a private seller's ad is kept the same way, but only 90 days, and is also compared with other ads, to spot a photo copied from someone else's ad. Matching photos are shown as small thumbnails from our server, so your browser never contacts the other shop.
  • Where those prices, reviews, photos and shop IDs were seen: with each price, review fingerprint, photo fingerprint and shop fingerprint's account ID, contact or address above, whether it came from a page our server fetched itself or from a page your browser or app sent, from a quick or a deep check, and how many independent checks reported the same (a number). A quick check records these too, after it has answered; it doesn't look them up. Nothing says which pass, wallet or network reported what: to count each only once, the server keeps a one-way code of the pass and network for each in memory, for the current day only, under a key it replaces every day and never stores (a restart clears it too). In memory only, it also counts per network how many checks recorded something today, to limit them.
  • Ad fingerprints: for a private seller's ad, a fingerprint of its text (a 64-bit hash of its words; the text can't be read back from it), the platform, a one-way hash of the ad's address and the day, to spot the same ad posted many times. Phone numbers and e-mail addresses written in the ad are kept only as a keyed one-way hash, to notice one number behind many ads, and are never shown to anyone. No seller name, account, location, price or ad text, and nothing about who checked the ad. Kept 90 days after we last saw the ad.
  • Recent answers for agents: the answer to a paid check of a page our server fetched itself, under a one-way hash of the listing, for one day, so the same unchanged listing can be answered quickly. No address, caller or payment details.
  • Results you share: only if you tap Share this result and confirm the preview. Anyone with the link then sees the verdict in our public wording (no score, no quotes from the page), the product's title, the shop, the product link (without tracking parameters) and the day of the check, for 90 days; then it is deleted. Nothing about who shared it: the share is sent without your sign-in, and we keep no wallet, pass, IP address, time of day or view count with it. You get a delete code once (we keep only its hash) to delete it sooner. Shared pages ask search engines not to list them.
  • Requests from shop owners: what you send with the form on For shop owners (the kind of request, your site, your message and, if you give one, your e-mail address) and the day it arrived. No IP address or time of day. Used only to handle your request; deleted once it is handled, and after 90 days at the latest.
  • Usage statistics: to see how Unfret is used, the server writes one anonymous event for certain things that happen, with the exact time it happened and only these fields: what kind of event it was (a pass made, a quick or deep check, a payment, a tool listed or called on the MCP server, a step of the MCP sign-in); how it came in (the API, the MCP server, the browser extension, the website or the Android app); and, depending on the kind, a few fixed choices: where a pass was made and whether it is a developer pass or came with free checks; a check's tier, whether it was free, paid from a pass or paid per check over x402 (and on which network), whether it worked, was refused or couldn't read the page, and whether the pass is more than a day old; a payment's method (card, Solana or Base), what it paid for, its provider and its currency; the name of the MCP tool called. Nothing else: no pass, key, account or wallet (not even as a hash), no IP address, no browser or device details, no app or MCP client name, and nothing about the page checked (no address and no domain). Because each event keeps its exact time and Unfret has few users, an event is less anonymous than a daily total would be: someone who knew you did something at a certain minute could find that event. It still says nothing about who you are or what you checked. Deleted after 400 days.

For shop owners and service companies

Contact e-mail addresses, phone numbers and postal addresses on a shop's or service company's pages, and a service company's e-mail domain, are stored only as a keyed one-way hash, never shown to anyone, and used only to tell whether two sites share them, or, for a postal address, how many unrelated shops give the same one (our legitimate interest in warning shoppers about networks of fake shops, virtual offices and call centres posing as local businesses). They are deleted 180 days after we last saw them. A check of a service provider stays with the person who ran it: it can't be shared as a link. To object, or to have a site or contact detail removed, use the form on For shop owners or write to hello@unfret.net. A shared result about your page can be reported from the page itself: it is hidden at once until we review it, and what you write is kept only until then.

For private sellers

If someone checked your ad, we keep only the ad fingerprints above: no name, account or address, and nothing that says who checked it. A phone number or e-mail address you put in your ad is kept as a keyed one-way hash for 90 days, used only to tell whether the same number is behind many ads (our legitimate interest in warning buyers about ads posted in bulk). To have it removed sooner, write to hello@unfret.net.

Reporting a wrong result

When you press Wrong result? (in the app, the extension or through the API), you say which risk level you think is right and which findings are wrong or missing. A report always keeps the page it is about, so a person can review it: that check's page facts, page text and result. It never keeps who sent it: no IP address, no pass or account, no time of day (only the date). Personal details are removed in your browser and again on our server: greetings with your name, delivery and e-mail addresses, phone numbers, bank and card numbers, and everything after the ? in links. The checkout page is never kept.

To help the person who reviews it, a language model hosted by Scaleway in the EU (the same provider that reads pages for the checks) reads the report once, as stored, and suggests a label; a person decides. A report never changes a result. Once reviewed, the page and its label stay as an example for improving the checks; reports not reviewed within 30 days are deleted, and when the review queue is full, a newer report that tells us more may take the place of one that tells us less.

If you turn on contributing, your device makes a random contributor key and keeps it. It is sent only with your reports, never with a check. We keep a one-way code of it, how many of your reports agreed with the final answer (your points), and, only if you choose one, a nickname, shown on the public leaderboard only if you opt in. The link between your key and a page is removed once the page is settled, or after 30 days. You can delete your contributor profile from the app at any time; reports already sent stay, without any link to you.

What your browser keeps

This website sets no cookies. It keeps a few things in your browser's local storage, only what it needs to work, and only after you use the feature:

  • Your sign-in on the link checker and My checks: your Unfret pass or a wallet's session, until you remove it on My checks.
  • A new pass made on the checkout page, until you confirm you've saved its backup code.
  • Claim tokens for a card purchase, to confirm it once you are back from paying, for up to three days.
  • A note from earlier versions that this browser had its free pass (no longer used).
  • A date on which this browser made a developer pass on the page for developers, so it makes one only once (the pass itself is shown to you, not kept).
  • For one browser tab only (session storage): a wallet's sign-in on the checkout and wallet pages; and a link you paste on the home page, for the moment it takes to open the link checker, which removes it as it starts the check.

The extension keeps in your browser: your sign-in (a pass or a wallet's session), whether that sign-in expired, whether it has had its free checks, its settings and which version of its privacy notice you accepted; and, in memory only, the results for the tabs you checked and a phone sign-in in progress. Uninstalling it removes all of that.

Server logs and limits

Our server keeps no request log: neither the web server in front (Caddy) nor the proxy behind it that routes requests to Unfret's service records your IP address or which pages you ask for. The service itself keeps no access log either. Its own logs (what it did, such as a check, a payment or an error) leave out IP addresses and the addresses of the pages you check, and mask any that turn up in a message.

To stop abuse, the service counts requests per visitor network (your IP address, or for IPv6 its /64), as a salted one-way hash, in memory only: never written down, and the salt changes at every restart, so counts can't be linked from one day to the next. Cloudflare keeps its own logs, as it does for every site it serves.

Who else sees what

  • A language model hosted by Scaleway in the EU reads the page text, and a wrong result you report (as stored, to suggest a label for the reviewer). We send it nothing that identifies you.
  • Public registries (RDAP, crt.sh, the EU's VIES) and Wikidata answer the shop and brand lookups. They see the shop's domain, its VAT number, or the product's brand and the domains named after it, not you. For some deep checks Common Crawl (a non-profit in the United States, its copies stored on Amazon Web Services) is asked for earlier copies of the shop's home page, and the Internet Archive (United States) only when Common Crawl has none: they see the shop's domain, not you. The same goes for asking them since when the shop's address has been online. The EU's Safety Gate alerts (dangerous products) are a copy on our server: checking a product against them asks no one.
  • Card and iDEAL | Wero payments: Polar (Polar Software Inc., United States) is the seller of record and decides what it does with your payment details, e-mail and country, under its own privacy policy. It may keep them in the United States, Canada or elsewhere outside the EU, under the EU's standard contractual clauses; cards are processed by Stripe.
  • Crypto payments are Solana transactions, public on the blockchain like any other.
  • Your country: Cloudflare, which runs in front of this site, sees your IP address like it does for any site it serves. Cloudflare, Inc. is based in the United States and stores data mainly in the United States and the EU (EU-US Data Privacy Framework, with standard contractual clauses as a fallback); see its privacy policy. Our server gets only the country code it works out, uses it only to suggest a country for prices, payment options and VAT, which you can change at checkout, and never stores it.
  • When you pay with SOL or USDC you choose your country, for VAT. It may be prefilled from the country Cloudflare sees your connection coming from; your IP address is never stored. For our tax records we keep the day, the country, the amount, the VAT and the transaction signature of each purchase for ten years, as the law requires.

Nothing is sold. No affiliate links to shops (Unfret never earns from the shops it checks), no trackers, no ads. This website loads nothing from anyone else: its scripts, styles and typeface (Figtree) come from unfret.net itself, so no font service or CDN sees your visit.

Why we may use it (legal basis)

  • To give you what you asked for (a contract with you): reading the page you check, keeping your pass's or wallet's balance, card order and crypto purchase records, invites, the hand-off of a pass to the extension.
  • Because the law requires it: the tax records of crypto purchases (ten years).
  • With your consent, which you can withdraw: a result you share (delete it with its delete code) and a wrong result you report.
  • Our legitimate interest in warning shoppers and keeping the service safe: the fingerprints and price records above (shops, contact details, ads, photos, reviews, prices), the request limits, the country suggestion, the anonymous usage statistics, and handling a request a shop owner sends us. You can object (below).
  • What this site keeps in your browser is only what a feature you use needs, so it asks no consent for it.

Your rights

You may ask to see the personal data we hold about you, to have it corrected or deleted, to have its use restricted, to receive it in a common format, and object to any use based on our legitimate interest; where you gave consent, you can withdraw it at any time. Write to hello@unfret.net. We hold no names or e-mail addresses, so tell us what identifies your data: a pass's ID, a wallet's address, a shared result's link, or a site or phone number of yours. We answer within a month. Signing out in the extension or on My checks removes your sign-in from that browser; uninstalling the extension removes everything it stored.

If you think we handle your data wrongly, you can complain to the Dutch data protection authority, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, or to the authority where you live.
