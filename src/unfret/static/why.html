Why Unfret?

There's more than one way to find out whether a shop is legit before you pay: a free website checker, an AI assistant, or your own searching. Each has its strengths. This page sets out what Unfret does differently, so you can pick what suits you, or use more than one.

Questions to ask any checker

Does it read the page you're about to buy from?
Unfret reads the one page you choose, when you click Check: the listing, its prices, its countdowns, its small print.
Does it look at the seller, not just the marketplace?
On a marketplace, the seller is often someone other than the platform. Unfret looks at the seller behind the listing.
Does it show where each finding comes from?
Every finding in an Unfret result names its source, such as the page, the domain registry or the EU VAT register.
Who pays for it?
The people and AI agents who buy checks, and nobody else: no ads, no affiliate links to shops. How Unfret makes money.
How often is it right, and does it say so?
Unfret publishes how it does on labelled pages, false alarms included: How accurate is Unfret?

Compared with free scam checkers

Many free website checkers are quick and useful for a first look at a web shop you've never heard of. They often look at the domain: how old it is, whether it has a security certificate, how much traffic it gets, and whether it's on trust or warning lists. Some also collect what other shoppers report.

What many of them do

  • Rate the domain or the shop as a whole.
  • Say little about a third-party seller on a big marketplace, where the domain is the platform's, whoever the seller is.
  • Show a score; how much of the reasoning they show varies.
  • Are often paid for by ads or by affiliate links to shops.

What Unfret does

  • Reads the page you chose: the product, its prices, its reviews, its shipping and returns terms. For an EU shop that keeps its company details on another page, its contact or legal page too.
  • On a marketplace, looks at the seller behind the listing: whether it's the brand itself, where it ships from, the company details it gives. On a second-hand ad: how old the seller's account is and its ratings, as the platform shows them.
  • Gives reasons in plain words, each with its source: the page, the domain registry (RDAP), the EU VAT register (VIES), Unfret's price history.
  • Is paid for by the checks people buy. No ads, no affiliate links to shops, no money from shops.

Free, with no account. Quick checks and deep checks are free for shoppers, with no pass and nothing that says who you are. A quick check (up to {{quick_per_minute}} a minute) reads the page you chose by Unfret's rules, with no AI and no lookups elsewhere: discount tricks, countdowns and ticked extras, the seller named on a marketplace listing, missing company details, shipping and returns terms, and shops that share accounts.

A deep check is the closer look, for when it matters: it adds a language model's read of the page and its reviews, the domain's age, the EU VAT register, the price history and the product photo against other shops. Deep checks run on donations, shared out a day at a time: once a day's share is used up, they pause until the next day (00:00 UTC), and quick checks keep working. Support Unfret.

Compared with AI assistants

Today's AI assistants are capable. Many can open a link, search the web and run a script, and can look up a domain's registration or a VAT number themselves; they reason well. Asked well, a capable assistant can do much of a careful check by hand. If you already use one, it can be a good second opinion. Unfret adds data it doesn't have, and gives the same answer to the same evidence.

An AI assistant

  • Works from what it can find or open at that moment, and from what you ask it.
  • Its answer can change with how you word the question, and from one run to the next.
  • You write the prompt, and decide each time what to look at.
  • It may not get to see the page: some big shops turn automatic visits away.
  • What you send it is handled under that provider's settings and privacy policy.

Unfret

  • Adds its own records, built up from the pages people check with Unfret: the prices seen for the same product over the last 90 days, at this shop and others; product photos that turn up on other shops; shops that share an analytics ID, a payment account or contact details; reviews copied from shop to shop. And it matches products against the EU's Safety Gate alerts.
  • For shops and product pages, the verdict comes from a small scorer trained on labelled pages; for ads and tradespeople, from written rules. The same signals always give the same verdict, and how often it's right is published.
  • One click, and the same checklist every time: who's behind the shop, the product and the brand, reviews, prices and discounts, hidden tricks. Each finding names its source.
  • The browser extension and the Android app read the page on your own device, as you see it, so they work on shops that turn automatic visits away.
  • Text on a page can't talk the verdict round: it counts only through what Unfret measures, and text aimed at AI readers is flagged as such.

Looking for a Fakespot alternative?

Fakespot, the browser extension Mozilla described as analyzing the authenticity of online product reviews, shut down on July 1, 2025 (Mozilla's announcement). Unfret is a different tool, not a copy. It looks at reviews too: the same review word for word, reviews imported from other sites, and reviews it has seen on other shops. And it looks at the rest of the page and the seller behind it: who is selling, how old the shop's domain is, whether it names a company the EU's VAT register knows, whether a “was” price was ever really charged, and whether the product photo is used by other shops too.

It works on Amazon and other marketplaces, on web shops of any size, on second-hand ads and on the websites of tradespeople and local services, as a browser extension for Chrome and Firefox, or by checking a link on any phone.

Unfret remembers shops, not what you shop for

No account, no IP addresses and no history of what you check: the pages you check aren't stored, and they're never linked to you. Our server keeps no request log, and the limits against abuse count visitor networks only as a salted one-way hash, in memory (Cloudflare, in front of the site, keeps its own logs). About you, Unfret keeps a pass: a public ID, how many checks it has left and the day it was made, with the pass itself only as a one-way hash (or, if you pay with a crypto wallet, the wallet's address and its balance). What it keeps over time is about shops: the prices it has seen, fingerprints of product photos and reviews, when a domain was registered, and which shops share an operator. Buying checks or reporting a wrong result adds a few records; the privacy statement lists every one, and how long each is kept.

What Unfret can't do

Unfret sees the page, not the deal: it can't tell whether a parcel arrives or what a seller writes to you after you pay. It can miss a bad shop, and it can warn about a good one. It never calls a shop a scam: it says what stood out and where that comes from, and the decision is yours.
